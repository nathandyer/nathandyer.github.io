I'm overwhelmed by my book backlog, and I've been feeling that way for several months. I tried to chip away at it steadily, reading 20-30 minutes a day when I can, but when you keep throwing new books onto the heap it's hard to get your head above water.

I'd like to get to the point where I've read everything I want to read, and then when I find a new book I can just blaze through it and keep my queue short, the same way I do with movies, games, and TV shows.

I still have books I acquired as a teenager on my list! I suppose that begs the question, if I haven't read a book in over fifteen years, do I _really_ want to read it that badly?

In the spirit of getting through my backlog more efficiently, but still doing it at a pace where I enjoy reading and am not making it feel like a homework assignment, I've been using a few tricks to get through things faster:

## Audiobooks

I'm a huge fan of audiobooks. As a lifelong podcast addict too, I think I'm one of those people that can absorb just as much (if not more) from listening than I can from reading. There are some types of books or materials that work better in a purely textual form, but in most cases digging up an audiobook is a perfectly cromulent way to read something. And nevermind the people who tell you that audiobooks aren't actually reading!

I made a pass through my queue and pulled out around 10 books that have audiobook versions. In each case, I listen to the first 2-3 minutes to make sure I can tolerate the narrator and audio quality. So far, only one book has been outright rejected (due purely to the fact that the author read their own book and their voice is like nails on a chalkboard to me, though out of kindness for the author I won't name the book).

I find that I'm able to listen to audiobooks much more frequently than I can sit down and stare at a page of text, so I'm moving through the list much faster. It just slots into my day a bit better, and I can read while I'm also washing dishes or taking out the trash.

## Make peace with letting go of some of the books

Like I said in the beginning, some books I've had for decades without reading. At that point, if I still haven't made time for them, I think it's pretty clear that I don't actually want to read them all that much. Better to let them go, along with the mental clutter they take up.

Some books I know I _should_ read, but they're about subjects I either don't care about, or am tired of hearing about. I know that all my friends and colleagues might rave about it, but does it actually bring me joy or improve my life? If not, it's time to set it aside.

## Cliff notes

There are a handful of books that are purely informational, and are honestly just too damn long. The information they contain could have easily just been a blog post (in some cases, they actually started out as a blog post!). In those situations, I think looking up a detailed summary is probably sufficient. 

This is my least favorite method, and the one I recommend the least. Even for books where a summary captures most of what the author wanted to say, sometimes taking your time with a subject is important. It's also true that the author probably spent ages thinking about their subject, and chose almost every word deliberately, so you're robbing yourself of a thoughtful account of what they are trying to tell you.

In most cases, if you decide to go with the "cliff notes" option, it's probably worth reading back through the book later when you do have more time. But in rare cases, just breezing through a detailed (I recommend chapter-by-chapter) summary is sufficient.

## Stop adding new books

No matter how well you are moving through your backlog, if you keep adding new things, you'll never finish. 

With that in mind, I'm on a very strict "no new books" diet right now. I'm trying to avoid bookstores — best to avoid temptation. I'm staying away from sites like Goodreads or Bookwyrm. I'm not looking at the Libro.fm homepage, and I'm asking friends and family not to give me books for Christmas or birthdays (a request they're already accustomed to knowing I mostly just read ebooks nowadays).

If there is an absolute banger that everyone won't shut up about, maybe I'll consider picking it up, but the threshold is incredibly high. If you want to get through a backlog, you really can't keep piling books onto it.

## Burndown

At this point, I have 4-5 audiobooks left to listen to, 10 books to finish reading, and 18 that I have not yet started. If I am able to achieve a book a week (challenging, yes, but not impossible if also supplemented with an audiobook) then I anticipate reaching the end of the backlog mid-2027. That seems like a fairly long way off, but I know it's really not. 

Of course, I don't really expect to hit this target perfectly. I know I will fail. I'll add a couple books to the list (it's inevitable). I'll have a busy week here or there where nothing gets read. But at least now I have a set of tools and a solid plan to get there!
